# The Barber Who Dreamed of Amsterdam

I got a haircut today, and it turned into one of the better conversations I have had all year.

My barber talked a lot. He is young — younger than me — and probably earns a fraction of what I make. None of that mattered in the slightest. What came through was his passion: he genuinely loves the craft, and he has a plan. He wants to become a barber in the Netherlands someday. He spoke about it the way people speak about things they actually believe will happen.

I really wish him success.

There are always people like him out there — passionate, certain about their work, building toward something — and being around one of them is strangely restorative. His excitement was contagious in a way that no amount of well-compensated routine ever is. The whole thing cost me twenty-five thousand rupiah. Some things cannot be bought with money, and this was one of them. Honestly, I got more out of that half hour than from any psychologist I have ever paid.

I keep thinking about what actually happened there. A stranger, doing honest work with full commitment, told me his dream without embarrassment, and I left feeling more hopeful than I had in weeks. No advice was exchanged. Nothing profound was said. It was just two people, scissors and small talk, and yet something real passed between us.

It made me reconsider where hope comes from. We tend to look for it in big places — achievements, milestones, windfalls. But maybe it mostly travels through small encounters with people who love what they do. Passion is not something you can manufacture on demand; it is something you catch, briefly, from someone who has it.

I earn several times what he does, and I walked out of that shop envying him a little — or at least wanting whatever it is he has. Not the Amsterdam dream specifically, but the having of a dream you say out loud to strangers while doing your work.

Grateful for the haircut. Grateful for the reminder.
