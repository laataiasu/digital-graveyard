## **What Game Do I Really Want to Play?**

I keep asking myself this question again and again:  
_What game should I play?_  
Not just a game that looks smarter from outside, but a game I deeply _want_ to play, and maybe even win.

I believe there are many paths. Coincidentally, I lean toward what feels more rational, though deep down, I know “rational” is only a comforting word.  
It’s hard for me to stay too long in chaotic things. In the short term, chaos feels exciting, but mostly it brings anxiety, overstimulation, distraction.

And the most reasonable path, for now, is figuring out how to get enough capital to build something that truly matters.

---

### The fuel I really care about: truth

At the deepest level, what I actually want is understanding. The process of knowing what is true.  
Sometimes it doesn’t matter to me if it’s an “empty truth” that doesn’t bring money, or social praise, or material proof.  
But most of the time, truth is the only fuel that can spark something real.

I’d like to know where I stand: from myself, to my family, my area, my country, the world, even if my mental model is limited and incomplete.  
Yet there are two concrete levels that always remain real: myself, and the country I was born into.

---

### Who are we, really?

Indonesia is one of the biggest countries in the world.  
Yet almost no one really knows who we are, including many of us inside it.  
Outside, maybe we’re known for instant noodles, palm oil, or cheap coal.  
But if you look deeper, what we mostly do is import, buy, sell, and consume.  
And it doesn’t feel good enough for the long run.

And most people don’t know what kind of power structure truly shapes this:  
Who profits, who really controls, who pulls the strings quietly behind institutions, headlines, and campaigns.

---

### Why it matters

Sometimes it feels naive, maybe foolish, to think one person can map something so complex and messy.  
But I believe even a partial map helps.  
It shows that power has _structure_. That it’s not just random powerful faces on TV, it’s a network of ownership, influence, and alliances.

What frustrates me most is that “transparency” becomes a trend only when a scandal hits, then everyone forgets by next week.  
I want something more persistent.  
A platform that _remembers_ even when the public forgets.  
That connects data about people, companies, NGOs, affiliations, so we can see _who_ is behind what.

Because data _does_ exist, but it’s hidden, fragmented, locked in PDFs or behind expensive paywalls.  
The technology is there. What’s missing is the will, because real transparency often threatens the comfort of the powerful.

---

### My goal isn’t to destroy, but to reveal

I’m not naive enough to think that showing data and relationships will fix corruption overnight.  
But without truth, all we have left is narrative.  
And narrative without structured data is easily hijacked by whoever shouts the loudest, or whoever pays for the loudest microphone.

I don’t want to just be another critic, or another “data enthusiast” scraping PDFs in silence.  
I want to build something that lasts.  
Something that can show, in cold, structured data, how networks of power rule us, and how they change over time.

Maybe it won’t change everything. Maybe it won’t even matter that much.  
But it feels more meaningful than doing nothing.  
And maybe, it could help people see past slogans, see beyond individual candidates, to the invisible webs connecting them.

---

### The pain that sparked it

I’m trying to push for an open data initiative, to help build a truly data-driven society.  
But right now, it’s painful to even get the data.

From **[[Laporan Harta Kekayaan Penyelenggara Negara|LHPKN]]**, the asset declarations, details only exist in PDFs, impossible to process at scale.  
From **[[Administrasi Hukum Umum|AHU]]**, the corporate registry, no proper API, we need to make tickets and wait.  
From **[[Indonesia Stock Exchange|IDX]]**, data is hidden behind expensive paywalls that block investigative hobbyists like me.

I want to build something like LittleSis, but tailored to Indonesia, and more technical.  
So people can see networks: the ownership, the companies, the NGOs, the affiliations, the families.  
So people realize that sometimes our everyday choices and consumption empower the same elites we complain about.

My aim is to increase transparency. Because lowering transparency widens the gap to corruption, lowers accountability, and helps people in power stay invisible.

---

### More than just a market

Indonesia is huge, complex, contradictory.  
But if we keep consuming without producing, buying without building, forgetting without recording, we’ll stay just a market.  
Not a force.

I don’t want to accept that.  
At the very least, I want to see the shape of the system I live in.  
And maybe help others see it too.
