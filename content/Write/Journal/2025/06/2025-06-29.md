#### 1. **Mansur al-Hallāj (d. 922 CE)**

One of the most controversial mystics in Islamic history, al-Hallāj is remembered for his infamous statement **“Ana al-Ḥaqq”** ("I am the Truth"), for which he was executed.  
In his writings, al-Hallāj occasionally **sympathizes with Iblis**. In his _Tawasin_ (a collection of mystical treatises), there's a chapter called _**Ṭā-Sīn al-Iblīs**_, where he presents Iblis as a **paradoxical figure of loyalty**. He says Iblis refused to bow to Adam **not out of disobedience**, but because he refused to bow to any other than God.

> “He [Iblis] refused to bow to other-than-Him, and he was burned forever. And if he had bowed, he would have perished.”  
> Here, al-Hallāj plays with paradox: Iblis _disobeys_ the command of God, but only _to stay obedient_ to God’s uniqueness (_tawḥīd_). This is not endorsement--it’s a mystical reflection on love and loyalty taken to its extreme.
