# If You Love Her, Why Don’t You Post Her?
"Why don’t you perform your love so we can validate it?"

That question sounds simple. It isn’t.

It assumes that love must be visible, legible, and publicly verified. That affection needs an audience. That intimacy, to be real, has to be performed.

I don’t think that assumption comes from malice. It comes from society.

René Girard argued that desire is mimetic, we want what others want because others want it. Žižek pushed it further: desire is something we don’t fully understand; we are taught what to want. Jung warned that when you don’t know who you are, society will tell you.

Marketing completes the loop. It doesn’t just sell products; it sells modes of existence. It tells you how to live beautifully in the digital world. Graduate. Climb the ladder. Travel. Eat well. Post it. Call that “living.”

And when you scroll through it all, you feel something close to nausea.

I once noticed a friend who had no social media. When we hung out, he was present, relaxed, unbothered. Another friend once asked why some of us never post stories on Instagram. Someone answered, “I don’t want to increase social envy.” That answer almost sounds religious, avoiding *kecemburuan sosial*, avoiding *kufur nikmat*. Some traditions teach that gratitude means showing your blessings. Others warn against displaying them.

Already, values collide.

---

## You Can’t Reconcile Irreconcilable Values

Think about what “normal” dating looks like now. Overpriced cafés. Shopping malls. Buying things you don’t need. Curating an Instagrammable feed. Constant communication that slowly erodes solitude.

There’s no moral high ground here. No right or wrong.

Life is just choosing which stats to max out with limited capacity. Whatever you pursue seriously requires sacrificing something else.

Even something as innocent as traveling isn’t neutral. Carbon footprints. Congested cities. Unsustainable café culture in highlands built on FOMO. In some cities, public transport works; in others, private vehicles are unavoidable. Time, energy, and resources get burned quietly.

Travel abroad? It costs money, money that could be pooled, saved, redistributed, or used for something less impulsive. But then again, what counts as “impulsive” is itself socially constructed. Desire is manufactured, then moralized.

This is the real clash: it’s impossible to have everything. Every choice creates a dilemma, a trilemma, or worse. People like to say, “Just balance it.” But balance is a comforting myth. In reality, you’re always choosing what to sacrifice.

---

## On Values

I don’t like declaring values as slogans. I distrust clean labels.

But I grew up with Islamic teachings, and even though I’m now closer to agnostic than devout, some of those values stayed. Justice. Love. Self-actualization, not as self-indulgence, but as responsibility. Reducing poverty, inequality, ignorance, corruption.

I still believe technology could help with that. And yet technology itself creates new clashes. Surveillance creates security at the cost of freedom. Enforcement sometimes requires force, but force always risks becoming tyranny. Dialogue doesn’t always work. Neither does power.

We already live inside contradictions: centralized control, decentralized systems, AI monocultures, religious law, digital tribes. It’s not a red-pill-blue-pill choice. Everything exists simultaneously, pulling in opposite directions.

Stability costs freedom. Freedom costs coherence.

---

## Independence

I’ve been alone for years. At first it was terrifying. Later it became peace. I called it freedom. The other side of it, if I’m honest, is chronic loneliness.

I don’t invest much in relationships. They feel high-maintenance, and I tend to think in terms of survival, safety, and life expectancy. I ask myself why I should live long, and then why not.

I try to be an outlier. I wonder if a life without deep social intimacy could still work. Sometimes it does. Sometimes it doesn’t.

Burnout comes. Addiction loops. Crashes happen. You stop moving forward and ask why you always need to go somewhere at all. Why not stand still?

Then, unexpectedly, something happens. A small, low-agency action. A random initiative. Serendipity. Looking back, you realize it mattered.

That’s how I met you.

Because we were distant, I felt safe saying everything. I floodlit my thoughts without realizing that you were also a human being with emotions. I didn’t expect connection. I didn’t expect depth. When it appeared, I wanted to hold it carefully, to hold your hand, to hug you, to share life. I wanted you to have the best the world could offer.

But I’m not where I want to be.

I have unresolved issues. I want intimacy without losing autonomy. Affection without ownership. Love without the erosion of freedom. I’m afraid of the process, afraid it won’t work, afraid it can’t be solved, afraid that hope itself might be delusional.

Positive thinking helps, but believing you can walk doesn’t always mean you’ll walk like everyone else. I hate that lack. I hate struggling more than I fear ending things. I procrastinate. I lose focus. I second-guess everything.

I think transactionally because I’m not omnipotent. Time and energy spent can’t be returned. I hate that too, especially because if I don’t spend them meaningfully, I’ll waste them anyway, jumping from one distraction to another, avoiding long-term commitment.

---

## Epilogue

You are a wonderful person. You deserve clarity, not ambiguity disguised as depth.

I don’t post because I don’t want to turn people into symbols or content. I’m afraid of being known. I know about the spotlight effect, I know most people don’t care, but still, visibility changes things. Caring often becomes a way to justify one’s own existence.

Humans are social animals. I resent that fact sometimes. It’s also the reason we survived.

I’m in process. There’s no guarantee I’ll arrive.

Don’t bet on someone who hasn’t convicted himself yet. Define your own standards and needs. If there’s a clash of values you can’t tolerate, leaving is not failure, it’s wisdom. Don’t stay hoping to fix someone. Most men, including me, are not worth that kind of unpaid labor.

So the decision is yours. Stay or leave.

From experience, most leave. I’ve learned to live with that. Loss can become familiar. You can survive it.
