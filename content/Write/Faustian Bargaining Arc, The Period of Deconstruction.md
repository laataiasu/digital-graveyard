There was a period when I wanted to erase my previous identity, my beliefs, and everything that had shaped me. I wanted to be free. Free from judgment, free from inherited fear, free from the gaze of other people. Something like the moral abyss in _Apocalypse Now_: going so far into the jungle that the old rules no longer apply.

But I realized that erasing yourself is not easy.

There is pain in the process. There is no guarantee that after tearing everything down, you will find something better underneath. You might only find emptiness, confusion, or another prison wearing different clothes. But one thing is certain: there is always something to learn.

I grew up being taught as a Muslim. There were many rules, many boundaries, many things that were allowed and forbidden. I also carry shame for things I did in the past. For a long time, I lived inside that moral structure. But then I talked to more people, read more things, saw more of the world, and something inside me started to shift.

It is difficult to explain the process. But at some point, I just wanted to reset everything.

I had never eaten pork before. Then one day, I tried it for the first time through GrabFood from Warung Kelengan in [[Semarang]]. I ordered nasi campur. I felt strangely nervous. Doki-doki. The driver seemed confused because my name is Muhammad and I was ordering pork. It was a small act, but symbolically it felt huge. It felt like crossing a line that had existed in my head since childhood.

My previous values also made me avoid banking and fintech. I wanted to stay away from the world of riba. But my work became unbearably boring. I could not see a future in it. So I started applying again on LinkedIn, almost out of doom and desperation.

If a sharia bank had opened a suitable position, maybe I would have happily applied. But there was nothing. And eventually, I started to feel that sharia banking and conventional banking may be the same soul wearing different faces. So I convinced myself to enter the ribawi world and test it directly.

There were three companies I processed with: Cermati Group, BRI Raya vendor, and Krom. I received offers from the last two. I chose Krom.

Previously, I used sharia accounts for all my banking and investments. Now I am maximizing interest. I use Krom, Aladin Syariah, and Superbank. I even bought BBCA stock.

By doing this, I might be cutting myself off from many relationships. My extended family is mostly conservative and PKS-leaning. Maybe they will see me as astray. My old communities, like rohis, may no longer feel like home. Maybe I will lose the kind of belonging I used to have.

I do not know what I will gain from all this.

Maybe nothing.

Maybe I am only trading one form of anxiety for another. Maybe I am too self-aware and people do not actually care that much. Maybe some people will accept me. Maybe some will quietly distance themselves. Maybe I will discover that I cannot fully erase my attachment to my previous identity at all.

But deep down, I do not hate the people who raised me. I do not hate the environment I grew up in. I am grateful for them. I still hope and pray for the best for them.

I just want to create my own way.

I want to test the hypothesis. I do not want to live only through prejudice, inherited assumptions, or secondhand fear. If there is a hypothesis, then maybe it should be tested. And even if I never find the final answer, maybe that is fine. Maybe life itself is just an experiment: a long process of testing, failing, revising, and learning.

There is a saying: _Tuhan sesuai prasangka hamba-Nya_.

God is as His servant perceives Him.

I hope that is true.

I hope God is not as narrow as my fear. I hope God is wider than my shame. I hope God can survive my questions, my experiments, my confusion, and my attempts to become honest.

Wish me luck.

Or maybe luck is not necessary.

Maybe I only want to stop wanting so much.
