## Part 1

## Living without Regret and Becoming an Entrepreneur

In this video, Michelle Klein discusses the story of a former student who, despite earning a seven-figure income and having a prosperous life, felt trapped and unfulfilled in his work. This case raises questions about what matters in life and how to avoid living with regret.

- Questions raised in the video:
  - What will be left when the counting is done in our lives?
  - Will what I've done matter?
  - Will who I made the journey with matter?
  - What about the quality of my relationships?
  - What about my prominence?
  - What about my legacy?
  - How can I avoid living with regret?

- A formula for living, inspired by Bojangles Robinson:
  - Work like you don't need the money
  - Love like you've never been hurt
  - Dance like nobody's watching

- Current economic vibrancy and the rise of entrepreneurial efforts creating new employment opportunities
- Most individuals may feel destined to become observers, highly-paid managers, investors, or advisors, rather than entrepreneurs
- Statistically, fewer than 35% of GSB graduates, 25 years out, classify themselves as having undertaken something entrepreneurial or been self-employed
- Discussion of two contrasting attitudes that may lead to different careers and lives:
  - TS Eliot's The Love Song of J Alfred Prufrock, describing an attendant Lord who is deferential, politic, cautious, and afraid
  - W H Murray's Scottish Himalayan expedition quote emphasizing commitment as the key to initiative and creativity

- Key attitudes observed in most entrepreneurs:
  - Unending dissatisfaction with the status quo and the belief that something better can be done
  - Healthy self-confidence
  - Responsible competence
  - Concern for detail
  - Tolerance for ambiguity

- Don't bring capital when aspiring to run your own business
- Entrepreneurs should focus on the pursuit of opportunities without regard to resources currently controlled
- Steps to become an entrepreneur:
  - Identify a dream or idea
  - Figure out how to buy time to establish yourself apart from your present job
  - Begin with evening energies and work towards full immersion in your new venture while remaining employed
  - Be willing to take a big step and leave one's present position to translate one's dream into reality
  - Entrepreneurship is a reasonable career alternative, but there are no external barriers—only those we internally impose
- Questions to ask yourself:
  - What would I do with my life if I knew I wouldn't fail?
  - Would I prefer someone else's company or my own?
  - Are the risks of the entrepreneurial path more controllable than the risks of employment?
- The decision to become an entrepreneur is challenging due to high expectations from friends, family, and oneself
- The risks involved in pursuing entrepreneurship should be seen as execution risks, where performance leads to reward

In essence, living without regret involves aligning your actions with your desires, taking risks, and making a conscious effort to control the risks you can. Becoming an entrepreneur requires identifying opportunities, having the right mindset, and being willing to take a big step toward your dream while keeping your existing job. Ultimately, choosing a career path should revolve around what set of risks you can best control.

## Part 2

## Questions to Consider Before Pursuing an Entrepreneurial Career

- **Defining Ideal Work Environment**
  - Partners
  - Ambiguity and risks
  - Peers and status
  - Power dynamics
  - Size and structure of enterprise
  - Importance of money
  - Culture (structured vs unstructured)

- **Assessing Personal Skills and Ambitions**
  - Identifying essential and non-essential skills
  - Determining whether to recruit a partner or self-improve

- **Evaluating Potential Jobs**
  - Daily routine and career path
  - Availability of a mentor
  - Opportunities for advancement
  - How the job impacts career goals and future transitions

- **Maintaining Entrepreneurial Perspective While Working for Others**
  - Keeping the entrepreneurial dream alive
  - The importance of consistently nurturing entrepreneurial spirit through learning and networking

## Risks and Rewards of Entrepreneurship

- **Risks**
  - Risk of failure, but not the loss of one's reputation or own money
  - Losing other people's money when investing risk capital
  - Becoming constitutionally unemployable after a failed venture

- **Rewards**
  - Freedom and personal growth
  - Creating something meaningful and having an ethical impact
  - Achieving financial independence and the opportunity for multiple careers

## Key Takeaways

- **Timing**
  - Ideal age range for starting a venture: somewhere between the present and late 30s
  - More entrepreneurs start ventures before age 40-45

- **Mindset**
  - Entrepreneurs have vision and conviction
  - Don't wait for the perfect opportunity; be proactive in seeking out opportunities

- **Importance of Compassion and Kindness**
  - Service to others should be a part of any definition of success
  - Strive to make a difference in areas where you are qualified and passionate

- **Legacy**
  - Memorials and buildings will not be the lasting impressions; focus on the impact you have on others and the values you stand for

- **Inspiration**
  - Be unafraid to strive for greatness, even in the face of obstacles and naysayers
  - You are equipped to lead a powerful life full of success, passion, and joy; give yourself the permission to live it
