#### 2. **Perspective for the Ambitious**

* **Framing with Sets**:

  * Human → Literate → College graduates → PhDs → Professors → Tenured.
* **Key Point**:

  * Climbing elite sets leads you to eventually become the **lowest performer in that elite set**.
  * Referred to as the **"left-tail dilemma"**.
* **Examples**:

  * Air Force pilot vs. fighter pilot vs. weapons school grad.
  * PhD holder vs. elite school professor vs. tenured faculty.
* **Ambition is Addictive**:

  * Always wanting more — wealth, status, recognition.
  * But it leads to dissatisfaction, even among millionaires.
* **Coping Strategy**:

  * Consider whether being in the *left-tail* of an elite group is actually less fulfilling than being *at the top* of a slightly less elite group.
  * Sometimes **moving laterally** or **changing direction** is the right call.
* **Core Message**:

  * Stay ambitious, but maintain **perspective**.
  * Your **self-worth** shouldn't be tied exclusively to constant upward comparison.

### ✈️ Pilot Training & Lessons on Vulnerability

#### Story 1: T-38 Night Navigation Incident (Feb 8, 1991)

* Training at March Air Force Base
* Drifted on takeoff due to wind (crabbing)
* Given a heading change to 150°
* Turned the wrong way (right instead of left)
* Came within 20 feet of the control tower
* Lesson: Even experienced pilots make critical errors — vulnerability is real

#### Story 2: Mid-Air Close Call (Feb 5, 1992)

* Instructor flight with Mike Wood (three feet wingtip clearance at 250 mph)
* Student flying solo lead turns aggressively and incorrectly — into them
* Instructor reacts with emergency maneuver: full forward stick, negative G’s
* Other aircraft passed 4 inches above
* Lesson: Even close formations with skilled pilots can go wrong — vulnerability again

#### Story 3: Bird Strike Incident

* Low visibility due to wildfire smoke (San Antonio)
* 14-lb turkey vulture barely missed head, hit tail
* Point: Environmental factors can’t be fully controlled
